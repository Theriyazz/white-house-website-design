# White House, Raopura — User Research

**Audience:** wedding and festive menswear shoppers in Vadodara and Central Gujarat
**Prepared by:** RDzinStudio · October 2026
**Status:** desk research. Not yet validated in the field.

---

## 1. Scope and method

### What this is

A desk study to shape the demo landing page for the White House pitch. It combines:

- White House's own public footprint (Instagram `@whitehouseshop_vadodara`, Facebook page, JustDial listing snippets, an older Site123 page).
- Competitor and neighbour activity visible in search (Vadodara menswear and ethnic stores advertising on Instagram reels; local sherwani rental accounts).
- The three existing project documents: *Pitch Plan*, *Pain Point Research*, *20 Websites*.
- General knowledge of how weddings are planned and shopped for in Central Gujarat.

### What this is not

No interviews, no store visit, no analytics, no sales data. Nothing here is a measured fact about White House customers.

### Evidence labels

Every finding carries one of three labels. Use them when deciding how hard to lean on a point in the pitch.

| Label | Meaning | How to use in the pitch |
|---|---|---|
| **Observed** | Seen directly in a public source during this study | Safe to state |
| **Adjacent** | Supported by evidence from a neighbouring context (pan-India bridal reviews, Gujarat MSME studies, the existing pain-point document) | State as "typically" or "across the category" |
| **Hypothesis** | A regional mental model that fits what is known but has not been checked | Ask the client; do not assert |

### One correction to the master plan

The master plan positions White House as a "groom & bridal" destination with women's and bridal collections on equal footing.

**Observed:** the store's own profiles describe it as a suits and menswear house: tailored suits, sherwani, Indo-western, Jodhpuri, kurta, "one stop destination for all men's wedding outfits", with outlets named at Raopura and Alkapuri. JustDial review snippets praise sherwani and suit stitching and "reasonable price".

The demo therefore leads with menswear and the groom's side of the wedding. Women's and kids' wear appears, but lightly, until the client confirms what is actually stocked.

## 2. Who shops here

Five proto-personas. They are composites built from category knowledge, not from interviews.

### 2.1 Harsh, the groom (24–32)

- Works in Vadodara, Ahmedabad or Pune; comes home on weekends to shop.
- Discovers on Instagram reels and Google Maps. Saves screenshots.
- Wants to look like the reference photo he saved, not "like every other groom".
- Has never bought a sherwani. Does not know what a fair price is, how long tailoring takes, or which accessories he needs.
- **Fear:** it will not fit on the day, and there will be no time to fix it.
- **Decides** the style. Does not usually hold the budget alone.

### 2.2 Bhavnaben and Rajeshbhai, the paying parents (50–62)

- Hold the budget and the calendar. Often dressing several family members in one trip.
- Trust shops they or their relatives have used before. "We bought your uncle's suit from there" is a strong reason.
- Prefer to call rather than type. Read Gujarati comfortably, English adequately.
- **Fear:** paying more than the neighbour did for the same thing; being rushed by a salesman.
- **Decide** where the family goes, and when to stop looking.

### 2.3 Meet, the wedding-party member (20–35)

- Brother, cousin or close friend of the groom. Needs outfits for two to four functions.
- Budget-aware; will compare buying against renting.
- Wants to coordinate with the group without looking identical.
- **Fear:** spending heavily on something worn once.
- **Decides** for himself, quickly, usually in one visit.

### 2.4 The Patel family from out of town (Anand, Nadiad, Bharuch, Godhra, Dabhoi, Halol)

- Travel 40–90 minutes to Vadodara because the range is wider than at home.
- Come as a group of four to seven, in one car, for one day.
- Plan the route before leaving: which shops, in what order, where to park, where to eat.
- **Fear:** a wasted trip — the shop is shut, does not stock the price band, or cannot alter in time.
- **Decide** before they leave home which three or four shops make the list.

### 2.5 Nikhil, the NRI relative (28–45)

- From a Charotar-belt family, living in the US, UK or Canada. Lands two to three weeks before the wedding.
- Needs several outfits fast, often for a family of four.
- Researches from abroad; wants to see range and prices before flying.
- **Fear:** not enough days for stitching and alteration.
- **Decides** quickly and spends more per visit than local shoppers.

## 3. Mental models specific to Vadodara and Central Gujarat

These are the assumptions people bring with them. A site that fits them feels obvious; one that fights them feels foreign.

### 3.1 "The market" is a place, and Raopura is in it — *Hypothesis*

For much of Central Gujarat, wedding shopping means the old-city belt of Vadodara: Raopura, Mandvi, Nyay Mandir, M.G. Road. People do not think "which brand", they think "which bazaar, then which shops". Shops are found by landmark. White House itself gives its address as "Near Raopura Tower".

**Design response:** lead the store section with the landmark, not the postal address. Show both outlets, since Alkapuri solves the parking objection for those who dislike the old city.

### 3.2 The shopping trip is a family day out — *Hypothesis*

Three to five shops in a day, with a meal in between. The decision on which shops make the list is taken at home, the night before, on a phone.

**Design response:** the page's job is to get White House onto that list. It must answer, above the fold or one scroll below: what do you sell, in what price band, for whom, and are you open.

### 3.3 A committee decides — *Adjacent*

The wearer chooses, an elder pays, and a sister or bhabhi has a veto. Photos are forwarded into family WhatsApp groups; relatives abroad weigh in. The existing pain-point document notes there is rarely a clean way to share a shortlist.

**Design response:** a "Share with family" action on every product card that sends the item name, price band and link through WhatsApp.

### 3.4 "Vyajbi bhav" — a fair price matters more than a luxury signal — *Hypothesis, with Observed support*

The praise in the store's own reviews is "reasonable price", not "exclusive". In this market, a hidden price tends to be read as "expensive, and I will have to bargain". A site that looks too much like couture can make the family assume the shop is out of reach.

**Design response:** premium craft, approachable tone. "Starting at" prices on every card. No "price on request". Warm ivory and gold rather than black-and-white minimalism.

**Risk to raise with the client:** the master plan's "private atelier" language may overshoot. Ask whether their customers would call the shop an atelier.

### 3.5 Fitting is the real product — *Observed + Adjacent*

Reviews praise stitching and expertise. Across the category, the most damaging complaints are about fit and delay, not design. Trust sits with the tailor.

**Design response:** name the tailoring service in the navigation. State alteration turnaround plainly. Offer "Book a fitting" alongside "Enquire".

### 3.6 The whole party is dressed together — *Hypothesis*

The groom's side buys as a unit: groom, father, brothers, nephews, with safa, mojdi and mala as add-ons. Colour is coordinated across the group.

**Design response:** a dedicated wedding-party section and a single CTA to plan the set on WhatsApp. This is the largest basket the store can win, and few local competitors present it.

### 3.7 The calendar runs on muhurat, not on months — *Hypothesis*

Wedding dates cluster in muhurat windows, broadly November to early December, mid-January to February, and April to May, with a pause during Kamurta from mid-December to mid-January. NRI weddings often fall in late December regardless. Navratri is a separate menswear peak in Vadodara, which hosts some of the largest garba grounds in the country. Diwali brings new-clothes buying.

**Design response:** occasion-led navigation. The announcement bar and hero should be easy to re-skin per season: wedding, Navratri, Diwali.

### 3.8 Buy or rent — *Observed*

Sherwani rental is advertised openly in Vadodara. For a guest or a groom on a tight budget, rental sets the price ceiling.

**Design response:** show an entry price low enough to make buying feel sensible, and say what buying includes that rental does not: fitted to you, yours to keep, wearable again.

### 3.9 Phone first, touch first — *Adjacent*

Discovery happens on Instagram and Google Maps. Closing happens on WhatsApp, a phone call, or in the shop. Few people will pay online for a high-value garment they have not touched.

**Design response:** no cart, no checkout. Every screen carries a WhatsApp or call action. The page must load fast on a mid-range Android phone on mobile data.

### 3.10 Local pride, in the local script — *Hypothesis*

Competitor reels address "Barodians" and write in Gujarati. The White House logo itself carries the name in Gujarati.

**Design response:** English interface with Gujarati eyebrows and occasion names. A Gujarati line in the pre-filled WhatsApp message.

## 4. Behaviour patterns: the purchase journey

| Stage | When | What happens | Who leads | Channel | What they need from the site |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Date fixed, 2–5 months out | "We need to sort the clothes" | Parents | Family conversation | Nothing yet |
| **Discovery** | 6–12 weeks out | Reels, Google Maps "sherwani shop near me", asking relatives | Groom, siblings | Instagram, Maps, word of mouth | To exist, look credible, load quickly |
| **Shortlist** | The week before the trip | Compare three to five shops; forward photos to the family group | Groom and sister | WhatsApp | Range, price band, shareable items |
| **Pre-visit check** | The night before | Is it open, where to park, do they have my size and budget | Parent | Phone call, Maps | Hours, landmark, call button |
| **Visit** | One day, several shops | Try on, compare, negotiate, decide | Whole family | In store | Not the site's job |
| **Fitting** | 1–3 weeks later | Measurements, trial, alteration | Groom and tailor | In store, WhatsApp updates | Clear turnaround promise |
| **Pickup** | Days before the function | Final trial, collect accessories | Groom or brother | In store | Reminder, accessory upsell |
| **After** | Wedding day and beyond | Photos, reviews, referrals | Everyone | Instagram, Google | A place to be featured |

### Patterns worth designing for

1. **The decision is made off-site.** The site wins or loses at the shortlist and pre-visit stages. *Hypothesis.*
2. **Two different users, one household.** A son who scrolls and a parent who calls. Both actions must be one tap away. *Adjacent.*
3. **Screenshots are the currency.** Product cards should read well when cropped: name and price close to the image. *Hypothesis.*
4. **Late-night browsing, daytime calling.** WhatsApp works at midnight when the shop is shut. *Adjacent.*
5. **Guests outnumber grooms.** For every groom there are many attendees who also need outfits. Most local sites speak only to the groom. *Adjacent.*
6. **Time pressure rises sharply near the date.** The last three weeks produce urgent, high-intent visitors, particularly NRIs. *Hypothesis.*

## 8. Validation plan

Two weeks, low cost. The aim is to move the hypotheses above to confirmed, adjusted or dropped.

### 8.1 Customer interviews — six, 30 minutes each

Recruit two recent grooms, two parents who paid for a wedding wardrobe in the last year, one wedding guest, and one out-of-town or NRI buyer.

**Guide**

1. *Warm-up.* Tell me about the wedding. Whose was it, and when?
2. *Context.* When did clothes first come up? Who raised it?
3. *Discovery.* How did you find the shops you considered? Show me on your phone if you can.
4. *Shortlist.* How many shops did you visit? How did you decide which ones? What nearly made the list and did not?
5. *The visit.* Who came with you? Who had the final say? What made you walk out of a shop?
6. *Price.* How did you know what was fair? Did you know the price range before going in?
7. *Fit.* How many trials? Anything go wrong? How close to the date did you collect?
8. *Group.* Did anyone else in the family buy at the same time? How was that coordinated?
9. *Rent or buy.* Did you consider renting? Why or why not?
10. *Reaction.* Show the demo on a phone. Where do they tap first? What do they ask?
11. *Wrap-up.* If a friend were getting married next month, what would you tell him?

### 8.2 Staff conversations — four

Owner, a floor salesperson, a master tailor, and whoever answers WhatsApp.

- What are the five questions you answer most often?
- What does a customer who buys look like in the first two minutes?
- How many people usually come with a groom?
- Which months are slow, and what sells then?
- What share of customers come from outside Vadodara? From abroad?
- What do customers misunderstand about price?

### 8.3 Mystery shop — five stores

Raopura and Alkapuri competitors, plus one national chain. Log response time to a WhatsApp enquiry, whether a price band is given, how tailoring time is explained, and what is asked of you.

### 8.4 Review mining

Read the full text of Google and JustDial reviews for White House and five competitors. Tag each comment as price, fit, service, range, delay or other. Count.

### 8.5 Synthesis

Affinity-map the notes. Mark every item in sections 3 to 5 as Confirmed, Adjusted, Not supported or New. Rewrite the pitch using only the first two.

## 10. Limits of this study

- No primary research. Personas and mental models are informed composites.
- Public information on White House is thin and partly dated.
- Regional observations in section 3 are drawn from general knowledge of Central Gujarat and should be tested with people who live there, starting with the client.
- Scores in sections 5 and 6 are judgements made to rank ideas, not measurements.
